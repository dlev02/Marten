# Transaction and balance imports

Open **Transactions → Import**. Choose a file, select its worksheet and header row, match the columns, and review the ready and rejected rows. Nothing is saved until you select **Import ready rows**. The dialog opens with a **What your file needs** guide listing every recognized column and a **Download template CSV** button; the same guidance is in the FAQ under “What columns does my spreadsheet need?”.

Recognized Monarch transactions show a compact summary and **Review all column mappings**, including merchant and original statement as separate fields. Owner is explicitly not imported. CSV does not show a worksheet selector; file settings remain available separately. Generic spreadsheets retain editable mapping controls.

Three file shapes are recognized automatically:

- **Family expense sheet** (Month, Date, Amount, Category, Notes). Headers are detected even after a blank title row. No merchant is required: the category labels the description, while the merchant is explicitly “No merchant supplied.” Notes are preserved. Full dates and Excel date cells work; day numbers use Month plus a year in the row or the explicit **Year for day-only dates** field. A year is never silently guessed. The supplied template is blank; it is recognized without creating phantom expenses.

- **Monarch Money transaction export** (Date, Merchant, Category, Account, Original Statement, Notes, Amount, Tags, Owner, Reviewed, Id). Expenses are negative in that file, so the sign option is preset. Merchant, category, account, notes, tags, review status, and Monarch's ID are all mapped; Owner is not stored because Marten workspaces are single-household.
- **Monarch Money balance export** (Date, Balance, Account). The dialog switches to **Import account balances**: each account label is matched to a Marten account, and every day's balance is added to that account's history so the net worth chart and account charts reach back through the file. Monarch lists credit cards and loans as negative balances; they are saved as the amount owed. For a manually tracked account (including one the importer created), the newest imported day also becomes its current balance unless a later day is already saved, so accounts no longer sit at $0 after a history import; connected accounts keep the provider's balance. A day's net worth sums the accounts that have a balance on or before that day, so an account without imported history does not contribute until its first balance; importing the full export, which lists every account for every day, gives a complete history.

## File and column support

- Excel `.xlsx` and UTF-8 `.csv`, up to 25 MB, 50,000 transaction or balance rows, and 100 columns. Headers can be on any of the first 50 rows. For older `.xls` files, save a copy as `.xlsx` first.
- CSV supports comma, tab, and semicolon delimiters, quoted fields, escaped quotes, multiline notes, and a byte order mark. Malformed quotes produce an error.
- Required columns: date, description, and either one amount column or separate money-out/debit and money-in/credit columns.
- Optional columns: merchant, category, account, notes, tags, reviewed, and a transaction ID. Without a merchant column, the description becomes the merchant name. Merchant names are limited to 120 characters; descriptions to 500 and notes to 10,000. Tags are split on commas, semicolons, or pipes (at most 30 per row, 60 characters each) and are created in the workspace when they do not exist yet, matched without case differences. A reviewed cell of Reviewed, Yes, True, Y, 1, X, or ✓ marks the row reviewed.
- Account labels are matched to Marten accounts by full name, then by the name without a trailing “(...1234)” suffix, then by those last digits against the account mask (a lone digit match wins; several need a name overlap). Category names match without case or repeated-space differences. The preview lists each distinct account and category under **Accounts in this file** and **Categories in this file**. Existing names are matched; missing names default to **Create manual account** or **Create category**. New account types and closed status are editable. New category names are preserved, with a suggested icon and an editable expense/income/transfer type. A fallback account is requested only for unmapped or blank account cells and is never silently selected. Blank category cells use Uncategorized unless another fallback is chosen. Closed accounts remain available for historical imports.
- A category named in the file is treated as the person's choice: saved rules do not replace it and it is recorded as an edited field. Other rule actions still apply.
- The family sheet defaults to retaining identical rows, because repeated category/date/amount values may be separate purchases. Its fingerprints also include the source category and notes. Retrying the unchanged file skips saved occurrences. Editing a row's notes or category changes its identity; review overlapping imports rather than assuming edited files replace earlier rows.

## Dates and amounts

The preview uses Marten's stored convention: **positive expenses, negative income and refunds**. If a bank export uses negative expenses, choose **Expenses are negative** to invert its signed amount column. Separate debit/credit columns must contain nonnegative amounts, with at most one nonzero value in each row.

Amounts accept numbers, US dollar formatting such as `$1,234.56`, and negative values in parentheses such as `(42.50)`. More than two decimal places, malformed separators, and unsupported currency text are rejected. Marten currently uses USD; importing does not convert currencies.

Dates accept `YYYY-MM-DD`, dates with a four-digit year in the selected month/day or day/month order, and numeric Excel dates using the workbook's 1900 or 1904 date system. Impossible calendar dates are rejected. Excel date cells may contain a time component; the date is retained. Formulas are not executed: only stored cell values are read, so recalculate and save the workbook if formula-derived cells lack cached values.

## Duplicates, retries, and balances

Without a transaction ID column the importer compares account, date, normalized original description, and amount. Identical rows within a file are skipped by default. **Keep identical rows within this file** preserves multiple occurrences when they represent separate purchases. With a transaction ID column (Monarch's Id, for example) the ID alone identifies the row: two identical purchases with different IDs both import, and a repeated ID is the only duplicate.

New destinations are created only after selecting Import, in batches of at most 100. Preparation is atomic per batch and idempotent by owned import label/name: retries reuse destinations, including renamed imported accounts/categories. A later failure may leave newly created destinations and earlier row batches in place; retrying is safe. New manual accounts start at a displayed $0 and no current-day balance snapshot is invented. Set their current balances separately; a transaction export cannot establish them.

Each row receives a stable hash and occurrence number, independent of the file name, sheet name and row order. Category-only expense fingerprints also include category and notes; other formats keep their existing keys. Repeating a spreadsheet import or importing an overlapping export skips keys already saved by this importer. The final result reports those skips.

**Bank-synced and manual twins.** A row that matches a transaction already on the same account from a bank sync or manual entry (identical amount, dated within three days, nearest date first and a matching statement description as tie-breaker) updates that transaction instead of creating a second copy: tags are added, review state is kept if either side reviewed it, notes fill in when the existing note is empty, and a category named in the file replaces a category the person has not edited. The existing row takes the import key so a retry skips it. The result reports these as updated transactions. The same matching runs in the other direction: when Plaid or SimpleFIN later syncs a purchase that a spreadsheet row already holds, the sync adopts that row (bank ID, bank date and statement text, provider source) and keeps its merchant, category, notes, tags, receipts, and review state. Same-amount purchases within three days at the boundary of a bank's history can still pair wrongly; the drawer's original statement shows what was adopted.

**Accounts created before a bank connected.** Connecting a bank creates its own account records, so history imported earlier into a manual account sits beside them. Open the manual account and choose **Merge into another account**: transactions, balance history, recurring schedules, saved reports, and investment records move to the chosen account in bounded steps; spreadsheet rows that duplicate a synced purchase there are combined as above (receipts move to the surviving row); the target keeps its own current balance and any balance it already has for a day; then the manual account is removed. For accounts created by the importer, Plaid automatically reuses the same account ID only when name, last four digits, account type, and currency match uniquely in both directions. Closed accounts, missing digits, ambiguous matches, and other connected accounts are excluded. This preserves history and annotations and lets the existing transaction matcher adopt overlapping spreadsheet rows. Other cases retain the explicit merge flow; SimpleFIN retains its reviewed account mapping and cutover-date rules. Future providers, including FinanceKit, still require their own integration.

Selecting **Import** uploads the validated rows as one JSON file and starts a server job (`imports.start`); an internal action saves them in atomic batches of at most 100 rows and approximately 400 KB of payload, records progress on the job row, reschedules itself before the action time limit for very large files, and deletes the upload when it finishes. The dialog shows the progress bar and can be closed with **Continue in background**; a small status card follows the job anywhere in the app and a note reports the outcome, even after a reload or in a later session. One job runs at a time per household; a job silent for 15 minutes is treated as abandoned. A failed batch leaves no partial transactions or empty merchants. Earlier completed batches remain saved; a retry skips those rows. Only ready rows are sent. Rejected rows remain excluded until the file or mapping is corrected.

Saved transaction rules run during import. Original descriptions and notes remain available. Importing transactions does **not** change account balances or historical balance entries. Use a balance export (the Monarch balance file above, or a Date/Balance CSV from an account's **Balance history** tab, which also accepts a multi-account file and keeps only that account's rows) when historical balances are needed. Balance history can be imported for connected accounts as well as manual ones; dates after today are rejected, and the provider's current balance and daily snapshot are untouched.

File reading and preview validation run in a disposable background worker, keeping the interface responsive. Superseded previews are cancelled; the table renders only 20 transaction rows at a time. The workbook is parsed in the browser and is not uploaded or stored as a file. Only the selected, validated transaction fields are sent to the authenticated workspace mutation. Account and category ownership are checked server-side, as are dates, integer-cent amounts, and text limits.

## Implementation and verification

- UI: `src/features/transactions/TransactionImport.tsx`; entry point re-exported by `TransactionForms.tsx`.
- Parsing and preview: `src/lib/transactionImport.ts`; SheetJS is dynamically imported for Excel files. The dependency is pinned to official `xlsx-0.20.3.tgz`, following [SheetJS installation guidance](https://docs.sheetjs.com/docs/getting-started/installation/frameworks/). Reading uses bounded rows and a selected sheet, following [parse options](https://docs.sheetjs.com/docs/api/parse-options/), and respects [Excel date systems](https://docs.sheetjs.com/docs/csf/features/dates/).
- Mutations: `transactions.importMapped` (rows with optional tags, reviewed, and categoryMatched; returns inserted, matched, skipped), `workspace.importBalances` (any owned account, batches of 100), and `workspace.mergeAccounts` (repeat until `done`). The dialog itself goes through `imports.createUploadUrl`, `imports.start`, `imports.latest` and `imports.acknowledge`; the job runs `internal.imports.run`, which calls the same row helpers (`importMappedRows`, `importBalancesForUser`) one batch per mutation. Cross-source matching lives in `findMatchingTransaction` in `convex/lib/transactions.ts` and is shared by the importer, the merge, `plaidInternal.ingestTransactions`, and `simplefinInternal.ingest`. Optional transaction matching and connection cutoff fields are described in the architecture guide.
- Focused verification: `npx vitest run src/lib/transactionImport.test.ts convex/accounts.test.ts convex/plaid.test.ts`. Covers real XLSX bytes, both date systems, quoted CSV, refunds, duplicate keys, file limits, account ownership, batch rollback, rule application, unchanged balances, the Monarch transaction and balance shapes (tags, review state, IDs, account matching by last digits, debt inversion, per-name choices), tag creation, enrichment of a manual twin, a Plaid sync adopting a spreadsheet row, the downloadable template, and the account merge.

Browser QA used a separate fictional **Morgan QA** sample workspace through Aside on September 10, 2026. A two-sheet XLSX with a title row produced two ready rows ($18.75 expense and -$5.25 refund), one rejected impossible date, and one skipped duplicate. Toggling duplicate retention changed the ready count from two to three and back without resizing the preview table. Import saved two rows. Retrying saved zero and skipped the same two existing rows. A quoted CSV with negative expenses was mapped to $23.45 expense and -$4.50 refund and saved two rows.

## Spreadsheet history and bank connections

Plaid offers **After spreadsheet history** for each imported account, showing the next date explicitly. With one imported account ending today or earlier, that choice is preselected; multiple accounts require an explicit choice. A sheet ending today starts bank transactions tomorrow. The date applies to all selected accounts in that connection, so their history end dates must agree. Future-dated spreadsheet entries are not used for automatic suggestions. This establishes a date boundary, not proof that the sheet has no gaps.

Prefer a clear cutover: import spreadsheet history, then choose **From today** or a custom start date after its last expense when connecting a bank. **Available history** imports what the provider supplies (bridge history is bounded to five years). Current balances still connect; the choice does not erase saved history. Plaid stores the cutoff on the connection and applies it to subsequent syncs while allowing corrections to already-saved bank rows.

Category-only entries are excluded from automatic cross-source matching. An amount and a nearby date cannot prove that “Groceries” means a particular bank purchase. Tied or truncated match candidate sets also stay separate. Other exports, including Monarch, retain existing matching behavior, so inspect unmatched accounts and overlapping history.

If both sources are already imported, use **Transactions → Filters → Source** with the account and date range to compare them. This filters the list only. Select overlapping rows (up to 100 at a time), open bulk edit, and choose **Visibility → Hide from reports** for the set you do not want counted. **Show in reports** reverses that decision. Original transactions are retained; source filtering is not an automatic reconciliation or a global source switch. Mixed-account sheets need explicit account separation or a separate manual history account.

## Starter categories

New workspaces include convenience stores, pharmacy, haircuts, public transit, video games, accommodation, flights, ferries, eSIM/roaming, rideshare/taxis, travel insurance, tours/activities, parking/tolls, and fuel. New and reset workspaces receive the full starter set automatically, organized into focused groups for housing, utilities, food, transport, travel, shopping, health, education, entertainment, family, giving and financial costs. Existing households keep their organization; revisiting Categories does not recreate categories they deleted or reset renamed or disabled choices. The importer preserves a file's distinct names (for example Hotels) rather than silently replacing them with a broader preset (Accommodation).

Design reference: Monarch's [import guidance](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/4409682789908-Import-Transaction-Data-Manually-from-Banks-or-Other-Finance-Apps) distinguishes account mapping from column mapping. Marten keeps an explicit review of destination creation and retains its own non-destructive retry behavior.
